Home gardeners want to help the bees, but feel isolated and unsure if they're making the right choices.

OVERVIEW

Overview

Home gardeners who want to help the bee population may have a gap in their knowledge, tools, or confidence to make this change to their gardens. They may need help identifying which local plants are best for attracting bees and how to maintain a bee-friendly environment. They can feel overwhelmed and uncertain about what to do and if their choices are effective.

Our app aims to simplify the process by providing easy-to-understand guidance and actionable insights that help users not only transform their gardens but maintain them, inspiring, educating, and empowering users through collective knowledge and encouragement. To efficiently move the service into the prototype phase and begin testing, we adopted an iterative design approach.

THE SOLUTION

Guidance to build, and keep, a bee-friendly garden

Welcome & Onboarding

A friendly start that sets one clear goal: create a garden that bees love. Users add their location, garden, and plants during setup.

Scan Your Garden

The camera scanner identifies the plants already in your garden and analyzes how bee-friendly it is, with no manual entry needed.

Your Garden at a Glance

Home shows your bee-friendly score, daily care tasks, and local gardening communities to share progress with.

Garden Analysis

A garden analysis page shows how bee-friendly your garden is and what to improve first.

Local Plant Catalog

A catalog of local plants helps you learn which ones bees love and add them to your wish list.

DEFINE

The Problem

Many home gardeners are interested in making their gardens more bee-friendly but often feel isolated or unsure if they're making the right choices. They lack a supportive community where they can ask questions, share progress, exchange tips, or celebrate small wins. Without social motivation or peer feedback, their engagement and long-term commitment may decline.

The Goal

The goal of BeeFriendly is to help the user transform and maintain a bee-friendly garden. It suggests what plants they can add to their garden and helps them set up a schedule for the upkeep of their plants. BeeFriendly also has a catalog of plants that educates the user on local bee-friendly plants.

RESEARCH

User Research

To guide our design decisions, we began by identifying our target user groups: neighbors, parents, and active or long-term community members. We then developed a standardized set of interview questions to establish a consistent baseline for our research.

With this framework in place, we conducted contextual inquiries with participants who fit our user profiles, observing their interactions and asking follow-up questions as they performed relevant tasks. Once all sessions were completed, we analyzed the raw data to extract key user characteristics and compile detailed activity notes.

From these insights, we developed user personas and scenarios that reflected real-life needs and behaviors, helping us map out user activities and inform the app's core features.

IDEATE

Design Charrette & Mood Board

As a team, we first established that this would be a two-channel service: a mobile app and a website, the channels most commonly used for apps and services like this. To brainstorm as many ideas as we could, we ran a charrette exercise where each of us quickly designed screens for the app and website. We then shared what we came up with and picked out the features and ideas we agreed were best for the service.

We also developed a mood board to capture the intended user experience. It guided the branding and visual feel of the service and served as inspiration for how the app would look.

EMPATHIZE

Personas & Scenarios

Lee Gliv · Seattle

A 32-year-old lab tech who loves gardening. It's his go-to weekend stress relief after a packed week managing lab duties and caring for his family. Lee is aware of the declining bee population and wants to make his garden more bee-friendly, but he has to go through too many websites, search multiple times, and has no clear guidance. After a colleague mentions BeeFriendly, he installs it, sets his location to Seattle, adds details about his garden, and gets recommendations for native bee-friendly plants.

Emily Owens · Chicago

A 37-year-old software developer and passionate gardener. Scrolling through #savethebees reels in early spring, she's inspired but overwhelmed. Searching online for bee-friendly gardening floods her with so much advice she doesn't know where to start. She wants something clearer and more structured, so she downloads BeeFriendly and sets her location, garden type, garden size, and gardening expertise.

DESIGN

Wireframes

With the personas and scenarios established, we designed low-fidelity wireframes centered around the app's core features, making sure they aligned with the user needs identified during research. These were tested through usability sessions focused on key user flows to uncover pain points and areas of confusion, and the insights directly informed our design refinements.

DESIGN

Mid-Fidelity

After establishing the core user flows, we moved to mid-fidelity prototypes to refine layout, hierarchy, and interaction patterns. This allowed for more realistic usability testing and iterative improvements while keeping flexibility for design changes.

TEST

Usability Test

The goal of the usability test was to find out how usable the app's features are. It was conducted on the mid-fidelity prototype with people who have a garden and want to make it bee-friendly. To keep each session consistent, we created a set of guidelines so the team could compare results and find common pain points.

Tasks

  • Purchase a plant
  • Create an account and complete the garden onboarding
  • Add a plant to the garden
  • Create a task for maintaining the garden

Findings & Recommendations

Once testing was complete, we grouped the results and identified the key issues affecting the user flow. Here's what we found and how we resolved each one.

01

Unnecessary information on account creation

Finding: Users thought garden size by square feet was unnecessary, and many didn't know why it mattered.
Recommendation: Removed this screen so users aren't confused by details they found unnecessary.

02

Easier account setup with the garden

Finding: Users wanted an easy way to add plants to their garden while creating an account.
Recommendation: Added plant setup to account creation, with a camera scanner to identify plants in their garden.

03

Confusing navigation

Finding: Users were confused about what the bottom nav bar buttons do.
Recommendation: Labeled the buttons to support the user flow.

04

Page flow after account setup

Finding: Users were unsure why they landed on a news and tasks page instead of their garden.
Recommendation: Created a garden analysis page showing how bee-friendly their garden is, and moved news elsewhere in the app.

05

Task notification clarifications

Finding: Users were unsure how the app would remind them of their tasks.
Recommendation: Added a section to set up push notifications for tasks.

06

Custom tasks

Finding: Users were unsure what to do when the task they wanted wasn't an option.
Recommendation: Added a way to type in a custom task.

07

App location

Finding: Users wanted to add their location during sign-up and see it in the app.
Recommendation: Onboarding now lets users enter their location or use GPS, and it's displayed on the garden and catalog pages.

08

Local plants

Finding: Users wanted to wishlist or learn more about plants in their local area.
Recommendation: Replaced the shop page with a catalog of local plants they can learn from.

REFLECT

Impact

By empowering individuals to create and maintain bee-friendly gardens, the app supports local ecosystems, strengthens pollinator populations, and fosters environmental stewardship. Through education, community engagement, and actionable guidance, it turns everyday gardeners into contributors to biodiversity and sustainable living. As more people participate, neighborhoods become greener and more resilient, one garden at a time.

What I learned

This project gave me hands-on experience across user-centered design, from contextual inquiries and personas to wireframes and usability tests. I learned the importance of iterating on real user feedback and how collaborative problem-solving leads to more effective, user-friendly solutions.
